The idea behind GLOW-FM

GLOW-FM combines a lightweight Tiny-VAE encoder with an Hourglass Diffusion Transformer (HDiT) architecture to perform efficient enhancement in a compact latent space. Trained with Terminal Velocity Matching (TVM), the HDiT restores low-light latents in a single step. Together, the compact architecture and single-step inference enable efficient generative low-light image enhancement.

  • EfficiencyEfficient generative enhancement. Compact latent flow matching delivers one-step restoration at low computational cost.
  • Terminal Velocity MatchingTVM for image restoration. Adapted to low-light enhancement and validated against alternative one-step objectives in matched comparisons.
  • GeneralizationStrong generalization. State-of-the-art cross-dataset results among the compared methods, with competitive image quality.

Abstract

Low-light image enhancement (LLIE) aims to recover well-exposed and perceptually faithful images from severely degraded observations. Recent diffusion-based generative methods achieve strong perceptual quality by leveraging powerful image priors, but their iterative sampling incurs substantial computational cost, limiting practical deployment. Despite recent efficiency gains in generative LLIE, high-quality enhancement with a single function evaluation and low overall computation remains challenging, particularly under diverse real-world low-light conditions.

We introduce GLOW-FM, a one-step latent flow-matching framework for efficient generative LLIE. GLOW-FM performs conditional transport in the latent space of a frozen Tiny-VAE and leverages Terminal Velocity Matching (TVM) to enable enhancement with a single function evaluation. Matched comparisons with alternative one-step objectives support TVM as an effective design choice, and, to the best of our knowledge, this is its first adaptation to image restoration and specifically to LLIE.

Extensive experiments on challenging real-world benchmarks demonstrate that GLOW-FM achieves competitive image quality and state-of-the-art cross-dataset generalization while requiring only a single function evaluation and 34 GFLOPs for the small variant. These findings highlight the potential of GLOW-FM to bring generative LLIE closer to practical deployment through single-step inference and compact latent modeling. Code will be made publicly available upon publication.

How it works

Method

01 Construct the latent path

Encode paired images with a frozen Tiny-VAE encoder. Perturb the low-light latent with Gaussian noise, then interpolate toward the normal-light latent.

\[z_t=(1-t)z_0+t\tilde z_1\]

02 Learn the displacement

HDiT parameterizes the two-time displacement model \(F_\theta(z_t,t,s)\), conditioned on start and end times. At \(t=s\), \(F_\theta\) is supervised as instantaneous velocity; multiplying by \(s-t\) gives the displacement \(f_\theta\).

\[f_\theta(z_t,t,s)=(s-t)F_\theta(z_t,t,s)\]

03 Match terminal velocity

TVM matches \(\partial_s f_\theta\), computed via JVP, to the stop-gradient EMA teacher’s velocity at the predicted endpoint.

\[\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\mathrm{TVM}}\]

Inference · one network evaluation

Encode → transport directly from \(t=1\) to \(s=0\) → decode.

\[\hat x_0=\mathcal D\!\left(z_1+f_\theta(z_1,1,0)\right),\qquad z_1=\mathcal E(x_1)\]

Additional results

Unseen devices

GLOW-FM-XL achieves 4.33 NIQE and 28.4 MUSIQ on LSD-U, the unseen-device split (Tab. 2 in the paper).

Cross-dataset generalization

GLOW-FM-S achieves a mean NIQE of 2.83 across DICM, LIME, MEF, NPE, and VV, the lowest among methods compared in Tab. 4 in the paper.

Measured inference speed

GLOW-FM-S runs in 53.8 ms at 1024 × 1024 on an NVIDIA H100 (Tab. 15 in the paper).
